01 — Position研究者としての位置づけ

From sensing internal states to designing better human experiences.

My work sits at the intersection of affective computing, physiological signal analysis, human–computer interaction, and kansei informatics.

Since joining Shibaura Institute of Technology in 2016, I have developed and evaluated ways to estimate emotion, attention, stress, arousal, and engagement from EEG, heart-rate variability, and other signals. The work has moved steadily from controlled experiments toward real situations: social media, rehabilitation, robots, aroma, fashion, packaging, manga, food, learning, and museums.

I am interested not only in whether an internal state can be classified, but in what that evidence means for the design of systems, media, environments, and cultural experiences. I work as an independent researcher and collaborator across computing, engineering, culture, and human experience.

Current highlight · Belgium × Japan

Museum experiences, measured in the museum.

With Sébastien Lugan, Karine Lasaracina, Midori Sugaya, and Benoît Macq, I studied how original art, a 180° immersive projection, and interpretive video produce distinct neurophysiological patterns of emotional engagement.

The project grew through ASEM-DUO mobility, fieldwork at the Royal Museums of Fine Arts of Belgium, and an invited presentation during the 2025 Osaka Expo. It received the Best Paper Award at IEEE PerCom EmotionSense 2026.
