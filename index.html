01 — Profile研究者について

Researching the relationship between physiological signals, emotion, and human experience.

My research examines how internal human states can be estimated and understood through physiological sensing, and how those insights can inform interactive systems and experiences.

I work across affective computing, human–computer interaction, and kansei informatics. My studies use signals such as EEG and heart-rate variability in both controlled and real-world contexts, including media use, museums, biofeedback, and human–robot interaction.

The broader objective is to develop human-centered technologies that recognize individual differences and contribute to more responsive and inclusive experiences.

02 — Research研究領域

Research areas

01

Affective Computing

Emotion estimation and interpretation using lightweight physiological measurements, with an emphasis on applicability beyond laboratory settings.

02

Physiological Computing

EEG, heart-rate variability, and multimodal sensing as methods for studying engagement, stress, arousal, and emotional change.

03

Human–Computer Interaction

Interactive systems, biofeedback, and human-centered technologies that respond to users’ internal states and individual differences.

04

Experience & Kansei Research

The effects of media, exhibition formats, cultural environments, and designed experiences on emotion and engagement.

Current research focus

Neurophysiological responses to museum and cultural experiences

Recent work compares emotional engagement with original artworks, immersive projection, and interpretive video in a real museum environment using lightweight EEG, contributing evidence for future inclusive exhibition design.
